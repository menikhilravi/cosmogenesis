The timeline · 22 chapters on one clock

From 10−36 seconds to 10100 years

Each chapter is pinned to its moment on a logarithmic clock, where every tick lasts ten times longer than the one before. Life and everything after it crowd into the last sliver of time, so the axis changes scale five times: at first light, at the birth of the Sun, at the first cells, at the present moment, and again for the far future.

  • Pin. The chapter’s moment on the axis, joined to its plate.
  • Thread. How long it lasts. Many run all the way to now.
  • Event. Something else that happened then.
  • Scale. The axis zooms in or changes unit here.

The same story on an ordinary clock

All of it in one year

Squeeze 13.8 billion years into a single calendar year. The Big Bang is the first instant of 1 January and now is midnight on 31 December, so each second of that year stands for about 438 years. Each strip below magnifies the end of the one above it. This is why the timeline above needs a logarithmic clock.

Under the hood

Every chapter is a working model of its subject, computed in this tab as you watch. These are the engines, from quantum fluctuations to a heartbeat, each with one of the equations it solves.

Each chapter’s Science tab has the full model: the equations, the parameter values with units, what is scaled down for the screen, and the papers it comes from.
